⚡ Get the movement map first

Sideways position

Use left and right movement to line up with usable tunnel space. Avoid continuing a correction after the intended route is reached; a different visual setting can make oversteering easy to miss.

The jump decision

Learn the jump input from the game’s instructions and test it on a readable approach. The movement may feel familiar, but Snow Rider’s takeoff cues are not a calibration for this tunnel.

🔷 Treat the tunnel as connected landing zones

A bright tile or platform can attract attention without being the best destination. Look for a landing zone connected to a useful continuation. A small patch may be reachable but leave a much harder next jump than a broader route nearby.

The tunnel view can change your sense of orientation. After a movement, take a fresh look at which surface is usable and where the next hazard sits. Continuing the old input because it worked a moment ago can turn a good landing into a new problem.

🌌 Give the new view a few ordinary runs

Start by recognising a safe route and following it without unnecessary detours. Once the view becomes readable, work on the jump that repeatedly causes trouble. That gives each restart a purpose more specific than simply hoping to react faster.

Do not assume that every power-up or mechanic listed by a game directory belongs to this exact build. Use the visible instructions and behaviour. The same title can appear around different descriptions, and the live player is the relevant reference.

🛷 What transfers from the mountain?

Looking beyond the nearest danger is still useful. So is deciding where a movement should finish before it begins. What needs relearning is the tunnel’s geometry and the feel of a jump. Keep those as separate tasks instead of treating an early fall as evidence that your sled skills disappeared.

For a more explicit wall-switching route challenge, try Run 3. For steering with no separate jump command, use Slope. The comparison page helps choose by the decision you want to practise.
